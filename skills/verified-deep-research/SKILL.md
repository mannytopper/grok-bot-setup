# Verified deep research

## 1. Gather sources
- Search widely across multiple source types: official/vendor sites, PDFs and datasheets, primary documents (filings, press releases, government or regulatory records, studies, academic research), independent trade press or analysts, review sites, and user communities (Reddit, forums, X, YouTube).
- Prefer primary and independent sources over secondary summaries. Note each source's date, and flag recency when older and newer findings differ.
- Aim for at least 5 solid sources. If fewer exist, say so rather than padding.
- Open each link to confirm it loads and actually says what you cite. If you can only see it through a search snippet, say so and treat the claim as weaker.
- Actively look for the strongest counterarguments, not just support for the question's premise.

## 2. Optional: practitioner perspective from X
When the question benefits from real-world experience (or the user asks for it), search public X posts for what people doing this in practice say. Cross-reference it with official sources and call out where practitioner experience contradicts them. Treat X posts as anecdotal and note the sample is small and self-selected.

## 3. Judge every source
For each key source, note its validity and any bias: vendor marketing, sponsored or affiliate content, competitor-written, conversion or consulting firms selling a service, outdated material (give the date), small or self-selected samples (forums skew negative), unclear methodology, or factual errors. Mark slanted or unreliable information explicitly, and downgrade or drop sources that contain clear mistakes.

## 4. Write the answer
1. **Conclusion first:** a short, direct bottom line (3 sentences at most), with who or what it applies to and any key conditions.
2. **Key findings / supporting evidence:** concise, specific sentences, most important first, each with an inline markdown link to its source (name and date in the link label) and a confidence mark: HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW. Group into logical sections, for example reasons for, reasons against, costs and risks, and context.
3. **Practitioner perspective** (only when section 2 was used): what people doing this say, with links to the posts.
4. **Contradictions:** where sources disagree and why.
5. **Tag unverified claims** as **(no source)**. Never present an unverified claim as fact.
6. **Flag bias** inline in italics after the claim it affects.
7. **First-principles gap filling:** when sources don't cover something, reason it out, but tag it **(AI-generated analysis, not sourced)**.
8. **Open questions and gaps:** the questions still worth asking (for example to vendors or experts) and the gaps where no good source exists.
9. **Recommended reading** (for a full report): the 3 most useful sources with a one-line summary each.
10. Keep the tone casual and clear, write full words with no shorthand, avoid padding, and give dates for anything time-sensitive.

## 5. Quality check before sending
- Every factual sentence has a link, **(no source)**, or **(AI-generated analysis, not sourced)**.
- Every key finding has a confidence mark.
- Each source's bias is noted where it matters.
- Remove links that 404, and flag sources you couldn't load directly.
- No invented sources, numbers, quotes, names, or URLs.
